How it works

  1. Drag to walk

    The phone screen works like a trackpad, and the map stays hidden. A finger moves the walker through the house, starting just inside the front door. A set path runs through every room, so it's hard to get lost.

  2. Feel what you touch

    Walls, windows, doorways, and stairs each have their own vibration. Carpet, tile, and hardwood feel different underfoot. With AirPods Pro, turning your head turns the walker.

  3. Ask out loud

    Hold the bottom of the screen and ask: "Where am I?" "How many steps to the kitchen?" "Take me to the front door." A guided tour can also narrate each room.

Every surface has its own feel

These are the patterns the app plays. Press play to see the rhythm. Android phones vibrate it too.

  • WallOne hard, crisp knock. The only full-strength hit.
  • WindowA glassy double ping.
  • DoorwayTwo quick, light taps.
  • Front doorTap, tap, pause, tap. A chime in 3D sound points the way to it.
  • StairsA ladder of taps, rising going up.
  • HardwoodThree light, dull ticks per step, fading.
  • TileTwo soft pulses per step, spaced apart.
  • CarpetA smooth swell with no hits.

What you could offer a blind client

A photo gallery and a video tour are both made for eyes. Haptix gives your client the layout itself.

The real layout
Rooms, doors, sizes, and floor types come from the listing's own floor plan, traced into the app.
Explored alone
Your client learns the house at their own pace, without someone narrating it for them.
Questions answered on the spot
Directions always come from the way the walker faces: "Front door behind you, 7 steps."

Haptix is an early prototype. No one can download it yet, and it hasn't been tested with blind buyers yet. We're looking for a few agents to try it with us.
