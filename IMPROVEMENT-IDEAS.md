1. “Take me to any room” — my top feature pick.
Let someone select “Kitchen” or “Bathroom” and receive guidance from their current position.
You already have room connections and guidance in the tour code, so there’s a foundation to reuse.
Extend the spatial beacon to the selected destination.
2. Directions that follow a walkable route.
Currently, “Find front door” points toward the entrance and reports straight-line distance—even when walls intervene.
The tour chooses the next doorway, but directions within a room still point directly at it.
Add routing around walls so “ahead, five steps” leads somewhere reachable. This becomes especially useful with irregular rooms and furniture.
3. Explicit turning and adjustable movement.
The avatar changes heading after sideways movement when the finger lifts. That could be hard to keep track of. Offer “Turn left,” “Turn right,” and “Turn around,” with a short confirmation. Also let users adjust movement sensitivity beyond the current normal/fine toggle. Test both control styles with blind users before choosing a default.
4. Give users control over narration.
The code currently discards requests while speech is playing. Someone pressing “Where am I?” can receive no response. Add pause, repeat, and an option for a requested description to interrupt narration or play next. Keep automatic room announcements lower priority.
5. A house overview before exploring.
Start with a short structural description: “Two floors. The entrance opens into the living room. The kitchen connects to the dining area. Bedrooms are upstairs.” Add an accessible room list with connections and dimensions. This gives users a framework for interpreting what they encounter.
6. Furniture and meaningful obstacles.
Fixtures currently trigger a haptic bump when entered, but they don’t block movement like walls do. Add optional furniture layouts with objects you can walk around: counters, beds, sofas, and tables. Clearly distinguish items recorded in the floorplan from an example furnished layout.
7. A practice room with active learning.
Your tutorial explains and plays patterns. Add a small practice space: find a doorway, identify a wall, and return to the entrance. This lets users learn the controls before navigating a whole house.
For the hackathon, I’d prioritize destination selection, walkable routing, and narration controls. Then demonstrate a concrete task: a user explores the house, finds the kitchen, reaches a bedroom, and returns to the entrance independently. Measure whether they can explain how the rooms connect afterward—that’s stronger evidence of your impact than adding more vibration patterns.
